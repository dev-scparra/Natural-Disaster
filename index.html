EARTHQUAKES · THE TŌHOKU CASE

Natural disasters — Part 1

A sudden release of energy the crust can no longer hold

An earthquake is the sudden release of energy in the Earth's crust, caused by shifting tectonic plates. That release sends out seismic waves that shake the ground above.

STRESS Plates lock instead of sliding, and pressure builds for decades.
RELEASE The lock breaks, the crust snaps, and energy radiates outward.
90% of the world's earthquakes occur along the Pacific Ring of Fire — a belt of faults and volcanoes running through Japan, Indonesia, Chile, and the U.S. west coast, recording over 81,000 quakes a year.

Off the Pacific coast of Tōhoku, Japan — March 11, 2011

The strongest earthquake ever recorded in Japan

MOMENT MAGNITUDE

Offshore, the Pacific Plate slowly dives beneath the Okhotsk Plate. When the plates lock instead of sliding, stress accumulates for decades — until it releases all at once.

In 2011, a 500-km stretch of fault slipped up to 50 meters in minutes, thrusting the seafloor upward and displacing a massive volume of water. That displacement created the tsunami.

Natural disasters — Part 3

Consequences don't end when the shaking stops — but survival is learnable

Most earthquake injuries come from falling objects, not collapsing buildings. Three simple steps, and one packed bag, greatly reduce the risk.

1 DropGet down onto your hands and knees before the shaking knocks you over.
2 CoverTake cover under a sturdy table, protecting your head and neck.
3 Hold OnHold on to the table leg until the shaking stops completely.
↑ Near the coast?Move to higher ground the moment shaking ends — strong shaking near the sea is nature's own tsunami warning.
